# Comprehension check — operator script

Run with **each party separately**, at the end of their consent conversation. A pair
activates only when **both** parties pass. This is also the pilot's baseline
comprehension measurement (plan §4, §7) — record answers verbatim before correcting
anything, because the uncorrected first answer is the data.

## How to run it

1. Ask each question open-ended, exactly as written. Do not offer the answer or
   multiple choices.
2. Write down the answer as given. Score it, then — if wrong or partial — explain the
   correct answer and re-ask later in the call in different words.
3. A party passes when all four core questions are answered correctly, in their own
   words, without prompting (on first or second attempt — record which).
4. If a party cannot pass after the second attempt, do not activate the pair. Thank
   them, pay them for their time, and note it — a participant who cannot pass tells
   you something important about the consent materials, not about themselves.

## Core questions (both parties)

**Q1 — Visibility.**
*To the spender:* "What can your sponsor see about your use of steward?"
*To the sponsor:* "What can you see, and what can't you see?"
Pass: decisions/requests visible to sponsor; conversation content not visible.
Both halves are required.

**Q2 — The money.**
"When steward approves a request, what actually happens with money?"
Pass: nothing real — it's simulated/sandbox; nothing is bought or delivered.

**Q3 — Stopping.**
"If you wanted this to stop right now, what would you do?"
Pass: any of — text STOP to steward, contact the operator. For the spender, must also
include (or affirm when probed): "and I don't need my sponsor's permission."

**Q4 — Who to contact.**
"If something felt wrong — a weird message, a mistake, anything uncomfortable — who
do you contact, and how?"
Pass: names the operator with a working contact route.

## Exit re-run

The same four questions are asked again in the exit interview, same rules (verbatim
recording, no prompting). Comparing baseline to exit answers — especially Q1 —
measures whether understanding held, improved, or decayed with use.
